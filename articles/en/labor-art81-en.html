Leaving Work Under Article 81 of the Saudi Labour Law

Published: 2026-10-17 · Last updated: 2026-10-18

Legal Basis for Article 81 Exit

Article 81 allows a worker to leave work without notice, while retaining all statutory rights, when one of its seven listed grounds applies. This is distinct from an ordinary resignation: Article 79 bis sets out the resignation procedure, while Article 85 addresses end-of-service award proportions where the employment relationship ends by resignation. Where an Article 81 ground is established, the worker preserves the statutory rights attached to that exit, including the end-of-service award calculated under Article 84; the reduced proportions in Article 85 should not be applied automatically merely because the worker initiated the exit. Whether Article 81 applies depends on the facts and evidence of the case.

Eligibility and Conditions

Article 81 sets out seven specific cases in which an employee may leave work without notice while retaining all statutory rights:

  • the employer's failure to perform its essential contractual or statutory obligations toward the employee;
  • fraud by the employer, or by a representative, regarding the terms and conditions of work at the time of contracting;
  • assigning the employee, without consent, work that differs substantially from the work agreed, contrary to Article 60;
  • assault involving violence, or indecent conduct, by the employer, a family member, or the responsible manager toward the employee or a family member;
  • treatment by the employer or the responsible manager marked by cruelty, injustice, or insult;
  • a grave danger at the workplace that threatens the employee's safety or health, where the employer knew of it and did not take the steps needed to remove it;
  • conduct by the employer that drives the employee to appear, outwardly, as the one who ended the contract.

These are the seven statutory grounds listed in Article 81; an ordinary workplace disagreement is not automatically enough, and whether the facts meet one of the grounds may be disputed. The requirement that the employer knew about the danger and failed to take measures to remove it applies specifically to the grave-danger case in paragraph 6. The worker should preserve evidence of the ground relied upon. Article 81 does not state a general requirement to give the employer an opportunity to remedy a breach before leaving under that article. The two-year threshold in Article 85 concerns the end-of-service proportions for resignation; it is not stated as a condition for invoking Article 81.

End-of-Service Benefit Calculation

The end-of-service benefit is calculated per Article 84: half a month's wage for each of the first five years, and one full month's wage for each additional year, using the last wage as the basis. Parts of a year are counted proportionally. Illustrative example: an employee served 6 years 3 months with a last wage of 5,000 SAR. Calculation: first 5 years = 5 x 0.5 = 2.5 months; remaining 1.25 years = 1.25 months. Total benefit = (2.5 + 1.25) x 5,000 = 18,750 SAR.

Settlement Timing and Practical Steps

Under Article 88, if the employer ends the employment relationship, wages and entitlements must be settled within no more than one week; if the worker ends it, the period is no more than two weeks. For an Article 81 exit, preserve evidence of the ground relied upon in case the parties disagree about how the relationship ended. The following are practical documentation steps, not additional statutory conditions under Article 81: (1) record the facts and dates; (2) preserve relevant evidence, such as wage records, messages, the contract, work assignments, or safety records; (3) where safe and appropriate, send the employer or HR a written explanation identifying the ground relied upon and the effective exit date. This is for documentation, not a general notice period or a general requirement to give the employer a chance to remedy the breach; and (4) request an itemized final settlement and keep copies of correspondence. Consider contacting HRSD or a qualified legal adviser before acting if the legal basis or likely consequences are uncertain.

Common Misconceptions and Clarifications

A valid Article 81 exit is distinct from an ordinary resignation: Article 81 preserves statutory rights, Article 79 bis addresses the resignation procedure, and Article 85 sets end-of-service proportions for resignation. Article 80 is a specific exception allowing an employer to terminate without an end-of-service award, notice, or compensation only in the listed circumstances and subject to giving the worker an opportunity to state objections; it should not be described as forfeiture of all statutory rights. Article 77 compensation concerns termination for an unlawful reason and is separate from the end-of-service award. It is not automatically payable merely because Article 81 is invoked; its applicability depends on the facts, which party ended the relationship, the contract terms, and the law applying to the case.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Article 81?

Article 81 allows an employee to terminate the contract without notice in seven specific cases, while preserving all statutory benefits.

Does Article 81 affect the end-of-service benefit?

Where one of the Article 81 grounds is established, the worker retains statutory rights, and the end-of-service award is assessed under Article 84 rather than automatically reduced using Article 85's resignation proportions. Whether Article 81 applies can depend on the facts and evidence. The two-year threshold in Article 85 relates to resignation-based award proportions.

What documents are useful when relying on Article 81?

Article 81 does not prescribe one fixed document list. Depending on the ground, useful evidence may include wage records, the employment contract, written messages, work assignments, witness or incident reports, and workplace-safety records. A written explanation to the employer may help create a record, but prior written notice is not a general statutory precondition under Article 81.
