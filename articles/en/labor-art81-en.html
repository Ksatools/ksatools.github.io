Leaving Work Under Article 81 of the Saudi Labour Law

Published: 2026-10-17 · Last updated: 2026-10-17

Legal Basis for Article 81 Exit

Article 81 of the Saudi Labour Law grants an employee the right to terminate the contract without notice when the employer fails to meet essential contractual or statutory duties. This exit is distinct from a resignation under Article 85, meaning the employee is not subject to the two-year service threshold or the reduced benefit ratios that apply to resignations. Instead, the employee is entitled to the full end-of-service benefit as defined in Article 84.

Eligibility and Conditions

Article 81 sets out seven specific cases in which an employee may leave work without notice while retaining all statutory rights:

  • the employer's failure to perform its essential contractual or statutory obligations toward the employee;
  • fraud by the employer, or by a representative, regarding the terms and conditions of work at the time of contracting;
  • assigning the employee, without consent, work that differs substantially from the work agreed, contrary to Article 60;
  • assault involving violence, or indecent conduct, by the employer, a family member, or the responsible manager toward the employee or a family member;
  • treatment by the employer or the responsible manager marked by cruelty, injustice, or insult;
  • a grave danger at the workplace that threatens the employee's safety or health, where the employer knew of it and did not take the steps needed to remove it;
  • conduct by the employer that drives the employee to appear, outwardly, as the one who ended the contract.

These cases are exhaustive and do not extend to ordinary workplace disputes. The condition that the employer knew of the danger and did not remove it applies specifically to the grave-danger case. The employee should document the facts and the evidence of the case relied upon; Article 81 does not impose a general condition of first giving the employer an opportunity to remedy the breach. The exit stands even if the employee has less than two years of service, because the Article 85 thresholds do not apply.

End-of-Service Benefit Calculation

The end-of-service benefit is calculated per Article 84: half a month's wage for each of the first five years, and one full month's wage for each additional year, using the last wage as the basis. Parts of a year are counted proportionally. Illustrative example: an employee served 6 years 3 months with a last wage of 5,000 SAR. Calculation: first 5 years = 5 x 0.5 = 2.5 months; remaining 1.25 years = 1.25 months. Total benefit = (2.5 + 1.25) x 5,000 = 18,750 SAR.

Settlement Timing and Practical Steps

Under Article 88, the employer is responsible for settling all wages, the end-of-service benefit, and any other entitlements. Where the employee is the one who ended the employment relationship, as in an Article 81 exit, the settlement of all of the employee's entitlements must not exceed two weeks. Practical steps include: 1) submitting a written notice of exit to HR; 2) documenting the employer's breach; 3) obtaining a final pay slip; and 4) confirming receipt of the end-of-service benefit. Keeping copies of all correspondence helps resolve any dispute quickly.

Common Misconceptions and Clarifications

A frequent misconception is that leaving under Article 81 triggers the same benefit ratios as a resignation. In reality, the employee receives the full benefit per Article 84, regardless of service length. Another misunderstanding is that the employee forfeits rights if the employer ends the contract; forfeiture applies only in the specific cases listed in Article 80. Article 77 compensation is a separate matter: it is independent of the end-of-service benefit calculation and is not added automatically to that benefit, including in the Article 81 cases and in the worked example above. Whether it applies requires a separate assessment based on the facts, the contract and its terms, and the applicable law.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Article 81?

Article 81 allows an employee to terminate the contract without notice in seven specific cases, while preserving all statutory benefits.

Does Article 81 affect the end-of-service benefit?

No. The employee receives the full benefit as defined in Article 84, regardless of service length, and the Article 85 resignation ratios and two-year threshold do not apply.

What documents are needed to rely on Article 81?

Documents that prove the case relied upon, such as unpaid wage statements, a written notice of exit, and any supporting correspondence.
